PROFILE & CONTEXT

An encounter in Paris.
A continuing philosophical task.

What can Foucault offer someone trying to understand how to live?

Gao Xuanyang has worked extensively on European philosophy and contemporary French thought. According to the interview presented on the Chinese page, he attended Foucault’s lectures at the Collège de France during his doctoral studies in Paris between 1980 and 1983. These recollections provide the personal setting for a discussion of ethics, subjectivity and the aesthetics of existence.

The interview materials were supplied by Professor Gao Xuanyang. Gao Yuan selected and organised the Chinese excerpts and provides access to the original documents. This English companion introduces the four themes of that selection for international readers.

01

From the formation of subjects
to the work of the self

Theme · The relationship between Foucault’s studies of power and his later ethical inquiry

Gao Xuanyang reads Foucault’s later attention to ethical life as connected with his earlier investigations of power and knowledge. The emphasis moves towards the possibilities of acting upon oneself within the historical conditions through which one has already become a subject.

In this account, the self is approached through practices and relationships rather than treated as an independent starting point. Questions about truth, authority and ethical conduct meet in the ways people understand and transform their own lives. The later work therefore extends the critical inquiry into subject formation by examining what people may do with themselves.

02

A subject formed
through relationships and practice

Theme · What kind of self is involved in technologies of the self?

The interview distinguishes ethical self-formation from the discovery of an unchanging inner identity. Gao Xuanyang emphasises that subjects emerge within relations to themselves and to others, and develop through activities that take place over time. The person doing the examining and the person being examined never become wholly identical.

His discussion connects ethical life with the aspects of oneself that become morally significant, the grounds of obligation, the work of transformation and the kind of life towards which that work is directed. It also introduces an important qualification: practices directed at the self may operate within systems of authority. Their presence alone does not establish freedom.

Relation

Understanding the self through its relations, rather than as an isolated substance.

Practice

Attending to the activities through which a person takes shape over time.

Openness

Recognising the limits of complete or final self-knowledge.

03

Giving form
to a way of living

Theme · The significance of an aesthetics of existence

For Gao Xuanyang, the appeal of Foucault’s aesthetics of existence lies in its bearing on lived experience. His response associates aesthetic practice with an ability to step back from exclusively instrumental purposes, to reshape established ways of living, and to keep a sense of possibility alive.

Beauty is discussed in connection with the changing relationships between a person, other people and the surrounding world. The emphasis falls on the quality of these relationships and on the dignity of a life that is more than a set of functions. The interview presents aesthetics as a practical orientation through which living can acquire meaning and direction.

04

A philosophy that invites
diagnosis and self-examination

Theme · How should Foucault’s work be read?

Gao Xuanyang cautions against turning Foucault’s familiar concepts into ready-made explanations. On his reading, philosophical criticism calls for attention to particular situations and to the reader’s own assumptions; it cannot simply supply authority for an answer decided in advance.

He connects this approach with his own changing experience of reading Foucault after arriving in France. Ideas that initially seemed difficult became questions to be confronted in everyday existence. The interview closes this discussion by bringing philosophical reading into contact with the ongoing task of examining how one lives.
