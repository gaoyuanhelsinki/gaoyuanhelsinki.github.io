Deification
& Sanctification

Finnish Lutheran and Chinese Neo-Confucian Dialogue

Gao Yuan · Original publication in English
Dialog · 61(1) · 39–46 · DOI: 10.1111/dial.12705

Original publication ↗ Citation ↓
01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Different traditions of human fulfilment

This study brings the Finnish interpretation of Lutheran deification into dialogue with modern Neo-Confucian accounts of sagehood, self-cultivation and the unity of Heaven and humanity.

The article asks whether these traditions can be compared without collapsing their different understandings of ultimate reality and human transformation. Its proposed route is the transformation of emotional and spiritual life, described as the “deification of emotions”.

This page is a research overview of an article originally published in English, not a reproduction of the published text. “Sanctification” follows the article’s comparative terminology for Confucian sagehood; it does not imply doctrinal identity with Christian sanctification.
02 / FINNISH LUTHER STUDIES

Christ present in faith

The article traces Tuomo Mannermaa’s interpretation of Luther within the Lutheran–Orthodox dialogue. In this account, justification involves the real presence of Christ in faith and genuine participation, rather than merely an external description of the believer’s status.

Gao Yuan considers the themes of gift, favour, faith, love and the exchange between Christ and the believer. These supply the theological framework for discussing transformation and fulfilment.

The study also considers the patristic background of deification and the significance of Orthodox discussions of divine essence and energies. These form part of the historical setting for the Finnish approach, rather than interchangeable versions of one doctrine.

03 / MODERN NEO-CONFUCIANISM

Principle, mind-nature and relational creativity

Gao Yuan organises the discussion of modern Neo-Confucianism into three generational groupings. This provides a framework for tracing different approaches to self-cultivation and human fulfilment.

Principle and the cultivated subject

The first grouping includes approaches linking the ultimate principle with the nature of the self. Feng Youlan provides an important example of the relationship between metaphysical understanding and the cultivation of human life.

The centrality of mind-nature

The second grouping foregrounds moral mind and nature. The article discusses the 1958 cultural manifesto and the claim that the exploration of inward moral life is central to Chinese culture and the ideal of unity with Heaven.

Creativity and ren

The third grouping examines relational and creative interpretations, including Cheng Chung-ying’s engagement with process philosophy and Chen Lai’s account of ren. These resources become important for the article’s comparison of ultimate reality, inward presence and moral transformation.

The generational scheme is the article’s organising framework. It does not imply that the thinkers within each grouping share one uniform doctrine.
04 / COMPARATIVE FRAMEWORK

Convergences and an essential distinction

The article identifies three comparative themes: relational interpretations of ultimate reality; presence within human life; and inward transformation directed towards an ideal of fulfilment.

These similarities make dialogue possible, but they do not remove the differences between Confucian ontological approaches and Christian accounts of God, creation and grace.

A central distinction must be retained: Christian deification, as explained in the article, does not mean becoming a person of the Trinity, a constituent part of God or identical with God in substance. Participation and communion preserve the distinction between God and the transformed human person.
05 / DEIFICATION OF EMOTIONS

A new point of comparison

Gao Yuan proposes emotional transformation as a way of deepening the dialogue when substantial identity cannot provide a shared foundation.

Participation and renewed spiritual life

On the Christian side, the discussion concerns participation in divine life through union with Christ. The article connects this with transformed freedom, tranquillity and joy, while preserving the distinction between divine and human being.

Cultivation and the recovery of goodness

On the Confucian side, it considers the renewal of emotional life through principle, mind-nature and the recovery of goodness. The unity of Heaven and humanity is explored through the traditions of principle and mind.

The comparison therefore turns towards the qualities and orientation of transformed life. It does not claim that the two traditions have the same account of its source or ultimate ground.

06 / RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION

Beyond an equation of doctrines

Extending the Finnish dialogue

The article connects a theological approach developed in Lutheran–Orthodox discussion with Chinese Neo-Confucian intellectual resources.

Comparing pathways of transformation

It examines how presence, participation, self-cultivation and inward renewal function within different traditions of human fulfilment.

Proposing emotional transformation as a bridge

The “deification of emotions” is offered as a conceptual approach for continuing dialogue without assuming identity between God and humanity or between the traditions themselves.

Preserving philosophical and theological differences

The argument treats differences in ontology as an essential part of the comparison, rather than an obstacle to be concealed.
