Luther and Taoism

Early Interpretation, Love and the Possibility of Dialogue

Gao Yuan · Original publication in English
Dialog · 63(3) · 100–107 · DOI: 10.1111/dial.12857

Original publication ↗ Citation ↓
01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

How was Taoist love understood in Nordic encounters?

This article examines early Nordic interpretations of Taoist love and asks whether missionary assessments adequately understood the traditions they encountered.

Gao Yuan connects a history of translation and interpretation with a systematic comparison of love in Taoist sources and the Finnish interpretation of Lutheran theology. The study considers how misunderstandings can be reassessed and how dialogue may proceed without assuming that the traditions have identical foundations.

This is an English research overview, not a reproduction of the published article. Interpretative claims describe the author’s argument. Consult the original publication for quotations, documentary evidence and references.
02 / HISTORICAL TRANSMISSION

Chinese classics in Nordic religious contexts

The article surveys early Nordic reception of Taoist classics, placing Finnish encounters within a wider Scandinavian network of translation and religious interpretation.

The historical background includes European intermediary sources, Nordic translations and the work of missionaries with direct experience of Chinese language and culture. These different routes shaped how Dao, virtue and the ideal spiritual life were presented to new readers.

Translation is therefore examined as an intellectual and religious encounter, rather than as a purely linguistic transfer.

03 / EARLY INTERPRETATION

Toivo Koskikallio and the comparison of Logos and Tao

Toivo Koskikallio, known in Chinese as Wang Weiyi, is a central figure in the study. Gao Yuan considers his missionary and scholarly engagement with Chinese thought, including his comparison of the Johannine Logos and Laozi’s Tao.

The article examines assessments that contrast Christian love and an incarnate Logos with an impersonal or empty Tao. It asks whether such contrasts give an adequate account of Taoist benevolence, spiritual cultivation and exemplary figures.

Kaisa Nikkilä’s studies and original research materials provide an important part of the documentary background. The author critically revisits the early interpretations rather than simply endorsing them.

04 / FINNISH LUTHER STUDIES

Two kinds of love, gift and participation

The theological comparison draws on Tuomo Mannermaa’s account of human and divine love and the presence of Christ in faith. Gift, participation and the renewal of human life form central elements of this interpretative framework.

Gao Yuan also engages with Finnish discussions of love and grace, including work by Risto Saarinen and Miikka Ruokanen. These resources help distinguish the divine source of love from human self-directed desire.

The article discusses a particular Finnish interpretation of Luther. It should not be treated as the only position within Lutheran scholarship or as an unqualified consensus.
05 / RE-EVALUATING TAOIST SOURCES

Three dimensions of love

Benevolence and the metaphysical Tao

Drawing on Laozi and other sources, Gao Yuan challenges the assessment of Tao as a lifeless concept incapable of benevolence. The article explores its creative and transcendent dimensions as a possible basis for comparison.

Human passions and self-centred desire

The discussion distinguishes this metaphysical dimension from human desires vulnerable to distraction and worldly attachment. It examines how Taoist texts criticise wrongly directed desires and relate them to the loss of the Way.

Exemplary figures and ascending love

The article then considers sages, spiritual figures and religious cultivation. Gao Yuan argues that such figures should not automatically be dismissed as false idols: within the interpretative model developed here, they may represent stages or examples of spiritual practice directed towards an ideal life.

06 / MODERN DIALOGUE

Renewal without doctrinal equivalence

The comparison identifies possible connections between divine love in Lutheran theology and benevolence associated with Tao, as well as between the presence of Christ in faith and the article’s interpretation of Tao’s presence in human spiritual life.

These connections are proposed as openings for dialogue. Gao Yuan retains differences between the traditions’ ontological and epistemological assumptions, including the distinctive Christian account of sin and grace.

Similarities in spiritual transformation do not establish identity between Tao and the Christian God. The article’s comparison seeks better understanding of different traditions, rather than their fusion into one doctrine.
07 / RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION

History, textual reassessment and comparison

Connecting reception history with philosophical analysis

The study brings the historical transmission of Taoist classics into conversation with questions concerning love and spiritual life.

Reassessing early missionary judgements

It returns to Taoist sources to examine the adequacy of early descriptions of Tao and religious exemplars.

Developing a dialogue on renewed love

The article proposes a comparative approach centred on the transformation and direction of love, while maintaining differences between the religious traditions.
