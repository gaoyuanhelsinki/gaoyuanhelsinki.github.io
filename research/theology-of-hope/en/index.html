A Theology

Global Crises, Human Expectation and Intercultural Dialogue

Risto Saarinen & Gao Yuan

Original publication ↗ Citation ↓
01 / OVERVIEW

Why reconsider hope?

Risto Saarinen and Gao Yuan introduce the “Theologies of Hope” issue of Dialog by asking how hope should be reconsidered in changing cultural, scientific and religious contexts.

The editorial identifies three reasons for renewed reflection: global crises such as climate change and the COVID-19 pandemic; expanding interdisciplinary debates, especially in psychology; and encounters between Christianity and other religious traditions.

This page is a research overview of an editorial originally published in English. It introduces the jointly authored text and its thematic context, rather than reproducing the publication.
02 / THEOLOGICAL LEGACY

After Moltmann: continuity and new tasks

The authors acknowledge Jürgen Moltmann’s formative contribution to the theology of hope since the 1960s. Their purpose is not to dismiss this legacy, but to consider the tasks facing a new generation in the circumstances of the twenty-first century.

The editorial connects hope with contemporary discussions of reconciliation, memory and death, as well as the dangers of populism and neo-tribalism. It thereby places theological reflection within a wider field of social and intellectual challenges.

03 / UNDERSTANDING HOPE

Desire, possibility and temporal horizons

The editorial discusses a common philosophical account of hope involving both a desire for an outcome and a belief that the outcome is possible. It also notes the plurality of experiences and understandings that this account seeks to describe.

Hope in the present

Hope is not restricted to events that have yet to happen. Concern about whether it is already too late to respond to environmental damage also involves a desire directed towards present reality.

Hope directed towards the past

Restorative and nostalgic hopes seek the renewal of an earlier condition. The authors draw attention to the ambiguity of such aspirations, particularly when they enter political rhetoric.

This temporal breadth helps explain why hope cannot be understood solely as confidence in a future event.

04 / INTERRELIGIOUS DIALOGUE

Hope beyond a single tradition

The authors question the assumption that hope is prominent in Christianity but irrelevant to other religions. Traditions concerned with human perfection and an ultimate relation to heaven or the divine can also articulate distinctive forms of expectation.

Chinese traditions, including Confucianism, enter this discussion through their reflections on ideal humanity and ideal society. The editorial identifies this as an important field for dialogue concerning a shared global future.

The editorial opens a comparative agenda. It does not claim that Christian and Confucian understandings of hope have identical foundations or provide an exhaustive comparison of their doctrines.
05 / THE THEMATIC ISSUE

Connecting theology with lived crises

Writing as co-editors of the issue, Saarinen and Gao explain their aim of bringing traditional theological resources into conversation with different cultural and scientific contexts.

The editorial highlights the practical orientation of the collection. It discusses Antje Jackelén’s response to the pandemic in Northern Europe and points towards the continuing significance of environmental questions and dialogue with Chinese culture and religion.

The issue also includes Gao Yuan’s separate article on deification and sanctification in Finnish Lutheran and Chinese Neo-Confucian dialogue. That contribution is a distinct publication and should be cited separately.

06 / CONTRIBUTION

An interdisciplinary and intercultural agenda

Situating hope in contemporary challenges

The editorial connects theological inquiry with ecological crises, the pandemic and changing social conditions.

Opening dialogue with other disciplines

It brings philosophical and psychological approaches into view without reducing theological hope to a single general definition.

Including Chinese intellectual resources

The text identifies Chinese reflections on ideal humanity and society as relevant to discussions of hope and the global future.

Framing a collaborative scholarly conversation

Its role is to introduce and connect the thematic issue’s contributions. As a jointly authored editorial, its contribution lies in the agenda and framing it develops.
