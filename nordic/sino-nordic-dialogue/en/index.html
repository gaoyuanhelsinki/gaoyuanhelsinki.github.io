01 / Research Perspective

A region understood
through its sources.

This research brings intellectual history, translation history, religious history and cultural transmission into Nordic area studies. Its regional horizon includes Finland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Iceland, with particular attention to their distinct languages and traditions of knowledge.

Rather than treating Northern Europe as a geographical backdrop, Gao Yuan examines how translators, missionaries, philosophers, sinologists and academic institutions participated in the reception and reinterpretation of Chinese thought.

Finnish, Swedish, Norwegian, Danish and Icelandic materials help recover perspectives less visible in accounts centred on major European languages. The broader framework also asks how Nordic and Chinese traditions have entered into reciprocal intellectual dialogue.

What happens to an idea when it enters another language, intellectual tradition and cultural world?

04 / From Translation to Dialogue

More than the discovery
of a translation.

Sources open the inquiry.
Ideas give it substance.
Regional contexts give it depth.

Finding an early Nordic translation is a starting point. The larger task is to explain how a Chinese classic was selected, understood, misunderstood, reinterpreted and sometimes creatively transformed within another intellectual setting.

Translation is therefore studied as a historical encounter: an event connecting languages, religious assumptions, philosophical concepts and networks of knowledge.

By bringing documentary research together with intellectual history and area studies, Gao’s work moves beyond a one-way account of Chinese ideas travelling westwards towards the more complex question of how China and the Nordic world come to understand one another.
