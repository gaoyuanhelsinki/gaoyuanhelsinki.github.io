01 / Research Perspective

Understanding the Arctic
through regional knowledge.

Extending Nordic area studies towards Arctic questions, Gao Yuan brings historical culture, intellectual exchange, regional cooperation and strategic inquiry into a shared research framework.

The Polar Silk Road is examined through transport and regional connections alongside the histories, languages, institutions and knowledge traditions through which actors understand the Arctic and one another.

This approach connects Nordic and Arctic studies with China’s engagement in northern Europe. It asks how long-term regional knowledge can inform interpretations of cooperation, cultural exchange and strategic choices.

How can historical and cultural knowledge deepen our understanding of China’s engagement with the Nordic region and the Arctic?

Strategic questions

Use regional understanding to frame longer-term questions about the Polar Silk Road and Arctic engagement.

Beyond Routes

Histories behind
regional connections.

Language, culture and historical memory belong within the study of regional relations.

Shipping, resources and infrastructure are important Arctic topics. Gao’s research adds attention to the cultural traditions, social understandings and knowledge systems that shape how actors interpret the region.

Historical and linguistic inquiry can clarify assumptions that otherwise remain implicit in discussions of cooperation. Intellectual exchange and cultural transmission offer evidence of connections that precede contemporary strategic initiatives.

The Polar Silk Road thus becomes a meeting point for Nordic area studies, historical investigation and wider questions of Arctic cooperation.

04 / Culture & Arctic Perspectives

Longer histories
of cultural encounter.

Nordic and Arctic societies have their own languages, literatures, religious traditions and institutions. Close attention to these contexts is essential to understanding regional relationships.

The reception of Chinese classics in Finland, Norway and Iceland records connections that long predate present-day cooperation agendas. Rare translations, missionary scholarship and intellectual networks offer sources for reconstructing these encounters.

Gao’s related studies use a Nordic and Arctic-corridor perspective to connect the movement of texts with broader questions of regional knowledge and intercultural understanding. Nordic cultural histories and the Arctic’s diverse regional contexts are examined in their particular settings.

05 / National Social Science Fund of China
COMPLETED PROJECT · 21CZX044

Finnish Translations of Confucian, Buddhist and Daoist Classics in the Context of the Polar Silk Road

This funded research provides a documentary and intellectual-historical foundation for the wider research framework. It investigates Chinese philosophical classics in Finnish translation and the histories of interpretation and cultural exchange that surround them.
