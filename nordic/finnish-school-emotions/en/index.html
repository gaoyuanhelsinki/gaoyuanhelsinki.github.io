01 / Research Perspective

Emotion as a philosophical question.

This research examines emotions as a point of entry into the relations between soul, reason, virtue, will, love and human fulfilment. Rather than treating emotion as the simple opposite of reason, it asks how emotional life participates in ethical cultivation and spiritual transformation.

The studies move from Plato and Aristotle through Augustine and medieval thought to modern Finnish interpretations of Lutheran theology. Gao’s comparative work then brings love, participation and transformation into dialogue with Confucian, Daoist and Buddhist traditions.

How can passions become resources for virtue, love and a transformed life?

“Finnish traditions” here includes distinct strands of scholarship: historical philosophy and moral psychology associated with Simo Knuuttila, and Finnish Lutheran interpretation associated with Tuomo Mannermaa and subsequent scholars. This page presents Gao Yuan’s research framework, not a claim that these strands constitute a single institutional school.

02 / Intellectual Traditions

From classical moral psychology
to Sino-Nordic dialogue.

A connected field of inquiry, with attention to historical differences rather than a single linear history of progress.

01

Plato

Gao examines the development from soul–body distinctions to the tripartite soul and the constructive possibilities of emotion in later dialogues.

02

Aristotle

The relations between emotion, judgement, virtue and happiness bring moral psychology into practical ethical and political life.

03

Stoicism & Neoplatonism

Passions, the ordering of the soul and philosophical practices of transformation provide important contexts for late-antique thought.

04

Augustine

Will, love, passions and the two cities form a bridge between classical philosophical resources and Christian accounts of spiritual life.

05

Finnish Lutheran interpretation

Union, participation, gift, favour and the presence of Christ frame questions of love, deification and human transformation.

06

Dialogue with Chinese thought

“Deification of emotions” and “relational love” provide comparative approaches to Confucian, Daoist and Buddhist resources.
